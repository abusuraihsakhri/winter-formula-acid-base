Winter's formula

Estimate the expected arterial PaCO₂ in metabolic acidosis, compare measured compensation, and optionally calculate the serum anion gap and delta ratio.

Clinical use: An educational interpretation aid, not a diagnostic or treatment system. Confirm the primary disorder using arterial pH, clinical context, and laboratory results. Never enter patient identifiers.

Methods and interpretation

Expected PaCO₂ = (1.5 × HCO₃⁻) + 8 ± 2 mmHg

PaCO₂ below the interval suggests a superimposed respiratory alkalosis; above it suggests a superimposed respiratory acidosis. Use this relationship when metabolic acidosis is present; an isolated bicarbonate result cannot establish that diagnosis.

AG = Na⁺ − (Cl⁻ + HCO₃⁻)
Corrected AG = AG + 2.5 × (4.0 − albumin [g/dL])
Delta ratio = (corrected AG − 12) / (24 − HCO₃⁻)

The delta ratio is reported only when the corrected AG is greater than 12 and bicarbonate is below 24. Normal ranges depend on the laboratory and clinical setting, and delta ratios should not be interpreted in isolation.

Reference: Merck Manual Professional — Acid–Base Disorders.
